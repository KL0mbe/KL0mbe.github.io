quote: You call it stealing, i call it borrowing
date: 2026-06-04

“The road of theft is one of great learning and invaluable skills if traversed with care”

There is the age old saying of “good artists copy, great artists steal” when it comes to
all things creative. This saying as true as it is in the world of creativity has another
valuable truth to it that can be brought to fruition when it comes to programming.

But the mode of thievery I am referring to in particular is that of copying someone else's
design. When I set out to make a personal website — the very site you are reading this
on right now — I did what most people do and started looking at other sites I liked for
inspiration. Here I happened upon one
<a target='_blank' href='https://paco.me/ ' className='hover:text-hover font-normal' >Paco Coursey's</a> site Oh how I felt as if Zeus had 
answered my prayers for in his site I found all that I wanted, a clean minimal 
design, simple yet pleasing colors and all in all just a site that really spoke to me.

So I shamelessly started to copy his design. I measured sizes, margins and padding. I 
found similar colors and implemented my own version almost one to one, with only 
minor ui changes to match what I preferred. But very quickly once I got past the surface 
level stuff I faced challenges in making my site work as Paco's did. For while I had access 
to the finished html, css and javascript from the site itself, I did not have access 
to the source code. Add on top of this my incompetence with the inspect tool in Firefox 
and it was hard to fully grasp how he made certain things work the way they did.

You see while Paco's site was minimal in design, it had plenty of complexities to be 
appreciated once you looked a bit more carefully. He for example has a back button on 
all pages that is off to the left of the content in the middle — also poached in here as 
you can see — yet the content in the middle is not pushed or affected by this back 
button and is perfectly centred which might just sound like a simple absolutely 
positioned div, until you start minimizing the screen where you will notice that it is not 
absolute and that the other elements still interact with it. This then raises the question 
of how he accomplished this feat of a quasi absolute div that both does and does not 
affect the other content on the screen and it turns out to be a css grid trick. He also 
made a beautiful little clock for his footer that I too ripped off and can be seen in my 
footer at the bottom of this page, but this is not an import of any kind. He genuinely 
took a circle placed three lines in it and through css and javascript math managed to 
rotate and transform them to mirror an actual clock, ticking and all. Yet another little 
detail that is not appreciated till further inspection. He also also for his writings section
 — as you will see filched in mine — has a list view that renders all his writings and within 
 this loop also manages to dynamically render the year whenever a new year is reached, 
 utilising sectioned list views. 

“utilising sectioned list views” that right there is where ripping someone off presents 
itself with this additional value found in copying someone else's design. You see in 
pilfering Paco's design almost line for line I was introduced to concepts, designs and 
practices I did not even know about. Which is to say concepts, designs and practices I 
could not have implemented even if I wanted to as I was oblivious to them even existing, 
such as sectioned list views, and css grid chicanery. In looting his design not just did I 
have to reverse engineer each of these intriguing aspects of his site and figure out how 
to implement them myself, but also increased the breadth of my programming 
knowledge. Acquiring multiple skills and fine tuning existing ones all while building 
towards a wonderful end result that allows me to brag about my purloining skills to all of 
you, show you that the road of theft is one of great learning and invaluable skills if 
traversed with care and that this, truly is the value in ripping someone off.
